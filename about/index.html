About Honey Barrel Hunter

When I started my bourbon journey, I was fascinated by how something as simple as grain alcohol, oak barrels, and water could create such a diverse array of sensory notes across the nose, palate, and finish. It did not take long to realize that many of these notes are inherently subjective, and that they can be influenced by countless factors, from what I ate earlier in the day to the activities leading up to a tasting.

Because of this subjectivity, I decided early on to conduct all of my tastings in blind flights. The goal was simple: remove as much bias as possible and refine my sensory perception. While developing tasting notes came easily, and was a lot of fun, maintaining consistency proved more challenging, especially when revisiting the same bourbon across multiple blind tastings.

Although my tastings are not completely blind, since I work alone, I aim to get as close as possible. I select four bottles from my collection with similar proof points, pour them into glasses, and shuffle them several times before tasting. This process helps minimize the influence of labels, price, and preconceived opinions formed from reviews or online content.

For scoring, I use a modified version of the T8ke system, rating each bottle on a scale of 1 to 10 across three sensory categories: nose, palate, and finish. These are weighted at 30 percent, 50 percent, and 20 percent respectively. Since bottles are tasted multiple times, each bourbon’s composite score is an average across all tastings.

The term Honey Barrel simply means that the barrel the bourbon came from was something special, exceptional enough to rise above the rest. And the quest to find those barrels is one I hope never ends.

With Honey Barrel Hunter, my goal is to bring transparency and insight to the single barrel picks I get to experience and to show how they stack up against national releases. More importantly, I hope to shine a light on outstanding local barrel picks so fellow bourbon enthusiasts, whether just starting out or well established, can make more informed choices.
